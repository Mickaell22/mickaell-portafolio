## Context

**Quishing** (QR phishing) is a growing attack vector: QR codes in restaurants, signs and emails that redirect to malicious sites. Users scan without checking where they're going, because a QR code can't be read at a glance. qr-shield analyzes that URL before it opens.

This is my thesis project. It is **in development (`v0.1.0-dev`)**: what exists today is the detection engine.

## What it does today

A FastAPI REST service exposes `POST /api/v1/analyze`: it takes a URL and returns a verdict, a score, and the reasons behind it.

The L1 layer is static heuristics, each with its own weight:

- **Punycode** (`xn--`, 50 pts) — the most expensive one, because it underpins homograph attacks: domains that look identical to a legitimate one but use characters from another alphabet
- **Literal IP** instead of a domain (40 pts)
- **Abuse-prone TLD** (35 pts) — Freenom freebies, cheap-registration gTLDs, and the ones that collide with file extensions (`.zip`, `.mov`)
- **Excessively long URL**, over 100 characters (30 pts)
- **Known shortener** (25 pts) — a curated list of 15 domains

Hits are summed and the result maps to a verdict. All of it under test coverage.

## Deliberate decisions

The shortener and TLD lists are **static and hand-curated**, not an external feed. That's a decision, not an oversight: for the scope of a thesis, a short explainable list is worth more than a source someone has to keep alive — and it's documented in the code along with its upgrade path.

I also deliberately left large-country ccTLDs out of the suspicious list: the volume of legitimate traffic would generate too many false positives for the weight they'd contribute.

## What's missing

The design lays out L1–L5 layers — domain reputation, content analysis, a scanning client — which today are **roadmap, not code**. I'd rather put it plainly: the heuristics engine works and is tested; the rest doesn't exist yet.
