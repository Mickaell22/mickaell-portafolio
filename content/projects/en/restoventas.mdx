## Context

Week one of a personal three-week challenge, one technology per week. This one was React Native: a daily-use app to record restaurant sales, with its own NestJS backend shared across all three weeks.

The real problem: a waiter at peak hour doesn't want to tap through a screen product by product. They want to say *"two chicken plates and one large soda"* and keep serving tables.

## Voice-driven order taking

The full flow:

1. The waiter holds the mic and dictates the order
2. The recording **stops on its own** when it detects silence — an audio-level sample every 200 ms feeds a detector that decides when to stop (silence, no speech, or max duration). If there was never any speech, the audio isn't even uploaded
3. The audio goes to `/ai/transcribe` (Whisper) and comes back as text
4. The text goes to `/ai/parse-order`, where the LLM turns it into items alongside the business's active catalog
5. The items land in the cart and the waiter corrects whatever is off before registering the sale

## The decision that matters: don't trust the LLM

A model can return a `productId` that doesn't exist, a negative quantity, a duplicated product, or JSON wrapped in loose text. If that reaches the cart, the customer gets charged wrong.

So the model's output goes through a pure sanitization function before touching anything:

- Drops any `productId` that isn't in the real catalog
- Coerces quantity to a positive integer, with a cap
- Deduplicates products by summing quantities
- Extracts the JSON even when the model returns it surrounded by text

It's deliberately isolated from the framework, as pure logic, so it can be tested without booting the app. The same idea repeats in the cart: total calculation lives apart from the UI and has its own test.

## The backend doesn't trust the app either

`POST /sales` accepts only `{ productId, qty }`. Prices and totals are **recalculated on the server** against the database; the client can't propose a price. Same rule as with the LLM, applied at the other end.

## Tech stack

**Backend:** NestJS 11 with TypeORM (entities and versioned migrations) on PostgreSQL. Auth with Passport-JWT and a global guard, DTOs validated with class-validator, and rate limiting via throttler. Tested with Jest.

**App:** React Native 0.86 + Expo 57 in TypeScript. React Navigation, Zustand for session and cart, expo-audio for recording, and SecureStore for the token, hydrating the session before deciding the first screen.

## One bug that took a while

The "today" history drifted by a day depending on the hour. The `created_at` column stored a timestamp without a time zone: in Ecuador (UTC-5), anything recorded after 19:00 fell into the next day as far as the database was concerned. Migrating it to `timestamptz` fixed it — a reminder that dates are never just dates.
